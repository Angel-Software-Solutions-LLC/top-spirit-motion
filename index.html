Scope the decision first
The first useful deliverable

Scope the decision. Close the gates. Then build.

A short charter makes an opportunity discussable without pretending the answer, architecture or commercial shape is already known.

Question

What must be decided?

One business question, with the executive sponsor and business owner named.

Boundary

What is inside?

Users, process, data, interfaces and constraints—plus what remains outside.

Evidence

What earns the next step?

Baseline, success threshold, stop rule, decision date and accountable forum.

Five gates: accountable owner · approved access · measurable baseline · explicit boundary · agreed value and stop rule.
Output of scoping · not a pre-written proposal

Governance as adoption control
Make useful adoption repeatable

Governance is not a policy binder. It is the operating system for adoption.

Every use case

Owner · boundary · metric · stop rule

Someone is accountable, the permissible use is explicit, performance is observable, and stopping is designed in.

Every affected role

Literacy · sanctioned tools · support

People know what is allowed, how to challenge an output, where to get help and how the work will change.

Useful adoption = value people can repeat + control leaders can inspect.
Control and adoption are the same design problem
